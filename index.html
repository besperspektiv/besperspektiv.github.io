Robot cell

0.038mmmedian final error on a UR3e

Vision-guided precision positioning

Eye-in-hand positioning system for a Universal Robots arm. A Basler GigE camera on the robot flange reads a ChArUco marker board, and the robot corrects its own position until it is on target. I took it from choosing the hardware to handover, including the camera and light mounts for the flange.

  • 68% of 154 logged runs landed inside the 0.05 mm target
  • Calibration chain written from scratch: camera intrinsics, hand-eye transform and flange-to-marker offset
  • Cameras, lenses and lighting compared against budget, parts sourced, mounts designed and the workstation built
  • Moved to a UR12e in August 2026 with a new printed flange adapter

Precision positioning software

The code behind the vision-guided positioning cell in Robot cells: camera capture, ChArUco detection, the calibration chain and the visual servoing loop that moves a UR arm onto a marker within 0.05 mm. It starts at boot as a service that the robot's own program calls, with a web console on a tablet at the cell.

  • Hand-eye calibration refined as one problem: the spread of the flange-to-marker offset fell from about 3 mm to under 0.7 mm
  • Traced lost camera frames to Energy Efficient Ethernet silently dropping about 9% of packets
  • Auto-exposure meters the highlights, so marker corners never clip and bias the result
  • Every run is logged; ten written findings on the error budget are kept next to the code

ARCH-E computer vision

Computer vision for the ARCH-E combat robot, which became the running example in the computer vision course I taught in autumn 2025. I built the dataset pipeline, trained the detector and ran it live on an industrial camera, alongside lessons on calibration, tracking and depth cameras.

  • 41 phone videos split into about 11,500 frames, cropped to 4:3, with 405 labelled by hand and converted to YOLO with my own scripts
  • YOLOv8n trained for 100 epochs: mAP50 0.99 on validation frames taken from the same videos
  • Live detection on a Basler industrial camera stream
  • Webots arena with two simulated cameras matching the real 2.1 mm lens, after a first version in ROS 2 and Gazebo in 2024

Firmware and simulation

240generationsof genetic tuning in a simulator that runs the real firmware

Tont: firmware, simulator and auto-tuning

Software for Tont, my 2025–26 Folkrace robot. The ESP32-S3 firmware drives on five time-of-flight sensors and an IMU. A MuJoCo simulator compiles the firmware's own navigation code unchanged, and a genetic algorithm races robots against each other in it to tune the firmware's parameters.

  • Simulator matched to real race logs: 50 Hz telemetry, the 140 ms lag from steering to yaw and a fitted motor model
  • Profiles race in heats of four on 7 track layouts; the winner is picked in a separate contest on fresh random seeds
  • Failed distance sensors are recovered one at a time, and the IMU recovers after a restart, without stopping the race
  • CI builds the firmware and runs the C++ logic tests, 6 golden regression races and the Python tests on every push
  • Main board generated in KiCad from a Python script; Eerik-Hannes Matsina reworked its power supply

Startup product

20kpoints/sdrawn by the laser from two 18-bit DACs

Thermal laser shooting target

An automated shooting target for training. A FLIR Lepton thermal camera on a Raspberry Pi 4 sees each bullet hit as a sudden hot spot, and a galvo-steered RGB laser draws the target and marks every hit. Games include zeroing a sight and tic-tac-toe. In a team of 3, I was the main developer of the prototype: the Python backend on the Pi, the ESP32 laser firmware and the calibration between them.

  • Self-calibration: the system finds its own laser dot in the thermal image and fits a homography, about 20 s for a 3×3 grid
  • Flask API with 36 routes behind a mobile web client, reached through a Cloudflare tunnel; 65 pytest tests
  • Firmware plays precomputed points from a timer interrupt and delays the colour to match the mirrors' lag
  • Points stream from the Pi over SPI into a lock-free ring buffer; a host-side compiler limits velocity and acceleration
  • Shown on a static shooting range and on a driving target platform at the police academy
